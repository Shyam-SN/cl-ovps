The research question was simple: can a placement decision anticipate what clock-tree synthesis will build, then prove its power impact after routing? The answer became a staged flow that asks CTS directly, preserves clock polarity, and measures only final routed designs.

01

First, define what “power saved” means.

We kept this phase focused on dynamic power and reported leakage separately. OpenSTA measures cell internal power and net switching power using the routed SPEF plus a gate-level VCD for each workload.

MEASURED OBJECTIVEPdynamic = Pinternal + PswitchingLeakage is reported separately; it is not included in these reductions.

That separation matters. A clock cell can consume internal power even when datapath activity is low. Net switching, by contrast, depends more strongly on the workload and extracted capacitance. We therefore kept idle, typical, and peak VCDs paired with both the baseline and optimized routed netlists.

↗

Why idle shows the largest percentage on AES. The clock keeps toggling in idle, so clock-cell internal savings remain large. Active workloads add much more datapath switching to the denominator: the same order of clock-cell win becomes a smaller share of total dynamic power.

02

Make placement ask CTS what it will build.

Early experiments with HPWL, MST, centroids, and leaf-wire estimates could not reliably rank tiny routed clock branches. The decisive clock-power change was discrete: a small sink relocation could change a CTS balancing cell, while nearby moves often did nothing.

Stage 1 therefore screens legal placement moves with a fast CTS trial. For each candidate, the oracle observes the actual sink-to-branch assignment and selected clock cells, then scores total clock-cell internal power. It uses signal HPWL and setup/hold limits as guardrails. The scoring objective includes active clock buffers and inverters as well as dummy loads, preventing a “dummy-only” win from hiding a larger active-buffer penalty.

The placement oracle predicts the clock-cell event; it does not claim to predict every detailed-route coupling change. Tier 2 leaf-wire proxies stayed out of the placer after held-out tests showed unreliable ranking.

03

Restructure only clock paths we can prove.

Stage 2 traces clock sources and sinks through the OpenDB clock graph, recording endpoint identity and inversion parity. It accepts only safe buffer stars: no direct sink or port tap on the parent net, legal parent/child relationships, and a proven path to each endpoint. It then tries a parity-safe inverter substitution and checks polarity, timing, slew, placement, and clock-gating boundaries before committing.

This is a cell-topology change, not a lower-Vt strategy. Eligible clock buffers are replaced by characterized inverter masters, and balancing dummy loads are pruned when the timing checks allow it. The flow does not infer CTS membership from a cell name alone; ambiguous graphs fail closed.

04

Only routed measurements count as results.

Each Phase 1 result was routed, extracted to SPEF, and measured with idle, typical, and peak gate-level VCDs. The sign-off flow checks setup and hold, detailed-route DRC and antenna violations, VDD/VSS connectivity, VCD annotation, and Yosys logical equivalence.

PHASE 2 · EMPIRICALLY GUARDED

Extend the flow, but let evidence set the boundary.

We added three opt-in datapath passes. Each is fail-closed, emits its own manifest and trial evidence, and remains outside the default optimization path until it produces positive routed savings across the held-out designs.

3C

Sequential sizing

Liberty audit covers 69 sequential masters and all 69 clock/gate pin groups. Thirty replacement pairs pass the static non-increasing clock-capacitance filter. No eligible instances appeared on these benchmarks, so no flop was changed.

Complex-gate folding

270 Liberty functions are evaluated by truth table. Candidates must be local, single-fanout, pin-mapped, legal, timing-safe, lower-power, and LEC-clean. GCD had no mappings; AES and RISCV32I trial folds failed the timing floor or physical mapping checks.
